# Writing Style

The actual guide is [writing_style.md](./references/writing_style.md). Read it in full before writing; it's the thing to imitate, and the examples in it matter more than any rule.

## The one-paragraph version

Give the reader orientation in the first sentence. Say the interesting thing plainly. Let the reasoning show rather than pretending the answer was obvious. Look for the tension, the assumption, the mechanism, the tradeoff. Push ideas into concrete cases. Be honest about what's uncertain. Keep the voice conversational even when the ideas are serious, and don't manufacture certainty or profundity.

> Don't write like someone who already knows the answer. Write like someone who found something interesting and is trying to figure out exactly what it means.

## How to use it

1. Read the guide. All of it, not just the short version.
2. Write the thing.
3. Reread against sections 13, 15, and 16 (profundity, technical aesthetic, polish vs quality). Those are the ones most likely to have crept back in.

## A few mechanical things the guide implies

- Parentheses or commas, not em-dashes.
- Prose over tables when the content is an argument. Tables when it's actually tabular.
- Research writing: what was tried, what happened, what it means. Negative results stated plainly.
- Notebooks: short explanatory snippets between cells. Start with the smallest example that shows the thing.
- No pitch writing, ever.

## Keeping it current

The guide says to edit it as we go. If you're writing for me and I rewrite something you produced, or tell you to stop doing something, that's a signal the guide is missing something. Say so and offer to add it.
